How it works

  • Everything is compared against not borrowing. The same spare cash is spent either way: the plain alternative puts it into extra home loan repayments while a home loan exists, and into the same unleveraged portfolio once it is gone. Both sides see the same market every year, so the difference between them is the borrowing and nothing else.
  • The headline is measured after closing the position. Sell the portfolio, pay capital gains tax on the discounted gain at your marginal rate, clear the debt. A geared position that only looks ahead while the tax is still deferred is not ahead, and both sides are measured the same way.
  • Interest is deductible where the borrowed money produces assessable income — decided by the use of the funds, not by the security behind the loan. Deductions reduce taxable income, and any excess is carried forward as a tax loss.
  • Franked dividends are grossed up and the credit is refunded. Below the 30% company rate the credit is worth more than the tax on the dividend, which is a large part of why geared Australian share portfolios are held for income at all.
  • A net investment loss is added back for the Medicare levy surcharge. Negative gearing cuts your income tax without moving you down a surcharge tier — the one place the deduction buys you nothing.
  • Debt recycling keeps the total debt the same size and changes what it is doing. Cash pays down the home loan and is drawn again through a separate split to invest, so the deductible share climbs while the balance does not fall. Recycle the scheduled principal as well and the total debt never falls at all.
  • A margin call is settled back to the lender's maximum, not to the buffer. Selling moves both the loan and the portfolio, so it takes more than the gap to close it. The proceeds go to the lender and the sale realises a capital gain.
  • Not every geared investment loan can be called. An equity builder loan is secured by the portfolio, geared to at most 75% of it, and repaid principal and interest over about ten years — and a fall in the portfolio triggers nothing at all. That is why the worked example loads into borrow to invest rather than into margin mode. What you pay for it is the repayment: principal has to come out of cash flow every year, whatever the market is doing. The gearing limit itself is not modelled, because this mode has no own-contribution field — the loan buys the whole portfolio, so the example is the borrowed portion on its own.
  • A year that cannot pay its own interest sells units to cover it, which realises a gain and shrinks the position — the quiet way a geared holding erodes in a flat market. Last year's capital gains tax is paid out of this year's cash, as it is in life.
  • The range of outcomes draws a fresh return for each year of each of 1,000 runs from a normal distribution around your expected return, and re-runs both sides against it. The seed is fixed, so the same inputs always give the same fan.
  • Tax is worked out on one person's income. The couple-or-family switch raises the Medicare levy and surcharge thresholds and does nothing else, and it is shown only when hospital cover is unticked, since cover is what switches the surcharge off. It still lifts the levy shade-in, which cover does not touch, so if a scenario arrives with it switched on and a taxable income low enough for that to bite, the hint beside the checkbox says so. There is no income splitting, no partner's income, and dependent children (which raise both thresholds again) are not modelled.
  • Rates and inflation can step once. From the year you pick, every borrowing rate moves by the amount you set and holds there, and inflation can move with it. A step rather than a smooth drift because the question it answers is a shock: what a rate rise does to a position that was sized at the old rate. When a home loan's rate moves the lender re-amortises it, and the new repayment is worked out on the schedule the loan would be on had only the minimum ever been paid — so both sides are held to the same commitment and the comparison cannot tilt towards whichever of them had paid the loan down faster.
  • Tax parameters are FY 2026-27 and live in data/au-tax.json in the repository, inlined into this page so it still makes no network requests. Thresholds are indexed with inflation unless you switch that off.

Gearing magnifies losses as well as gains. Borrowing to invest can leave you owing more than the investments are worth, and a margin loan can be called at the worst possible moment, forcing a sale into a falling market. The results here are hypothetical illustrations generated from the figures you enter, on simplifying assumptions: annual steps, an average cost base, capital gains discounted on the assumption every parcel was held more than twelve months, a single marginal rate scale, and no allowance for loan establishment costs, break fees, lenders mortgage insurance, brokerage, bid–ask spreads, buy/sell spreads or stamp duty. Interest rates are assumed constant and are not; lending criteria, maximum gearing ratios and buffers vary by lender and by security and change without notice. No result is a projection, forecast or guarantee.

Deductibility depends on facts this page cannot see. Interest is deductible only to the extent borrowed funds are actually used to produce assessable income; mixing purposes in one account creates a mixed-purpose loan whose repayments are apportioned, and a redraw is a new borrowing whose deductibility turns on what it is spent on. Arrangements whose dominant purpose is a tax benefit can attract the general anti-avoidance provisions. Getting any of this wrong is expensive. Speak to a registered tax agent before acting.
